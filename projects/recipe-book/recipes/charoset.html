Charoset

“Charoset” (also spelled charoset or chorezos) is a sweet mixture eaten during the Jewish holiday of Passover.

What it is for Symbolic meaning: Charoset represents the mortar (clay mixture) that enslaved Israelites would have used while building in ancient Egypt. How it is used: During the Passover Seder, charoset is part of the ritual foods on the plate, and it’s eaten as part of the story recounting the Exodus from Egypt. Why it is sweet Even though it symbolizes something associated with hard labor, it’s made sweet because it’s connected to the holiday’s broader theme: remembering suffering while celebrating freedom.

🍎 Classic Charoset (Apple, Walnut & Wine) 🧾 Ingredients 1/4 cup raisins 1/4 cup chopped dates 4 tablespoons red wine 2 green apples, peeled and chopped(about 3 cups) 1/2 cup chopped walnuts(about 4 ounces) 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon 1 to 2 tablespoons sugar 🔪 Instructions In a small bowl, soak raisins and dates in red wine for several hours or overnight. In a food processor with a metal blade or in a mixing bowl, combine the ingredients together. The consistency will be course.
