Charoset

“Charoset” (also spelled charoset or chorezos) is a sweet mixture eaten during the Jewish holiday of Passover.

What it is for Symbolic meaning: Charoset represents the mortar (clay mixture) that enslaved Israelites would have used while building in ancient Egypt. How it is used: During the Passover Seder, charoset is part of the ritual foods on the plate, and it’s eaten as part of the story recounting the Exodus from Egypt. Why it is sweet Even though it symbolizes something associated with hard labor, it’s made sweet because it’s connected to the holiday’s broader theme: remembering suffering while celebrating freedom.

🍎 Classic Charoset (Apple, Walnut & Wine) 🧾 Ingredients 4 medium apples (a mix of sweet and tart), 1 cup walnuts, finely chopped, 3–4 tbsp sweet red wine (or grape juice), 1–2 tbsp honey, 1 tsp cinnamon, Optional: pinch of nutmeg, Optional: 2–3 tbsp raisins 🔪 Instructions Peel and finely chop the apples (or pulse lightly in a food processor). Add the chopped walnuts and mix. Stir in the wine (or grape juice) and honey. Add cinnamon (and nutmeg if using). Fold in raisins if desired. Let sit for 30–60 minutes before serving to develop flavor. 💡 Kitchen Notes Aim for a coarse, mortar-like texture, not a puree Adjust sweetness and liquid to taste Best made a few hours ahead—flavor deepens over time
