Challah

description

“Challah” (also spelled challah/chalá) is a traditional Jewish braided bread, most commonly eaten on Shabbat (Sabbath) and Jewish holidays. Challah French toast carries Jewish significance because it transforms challah, the braided bread traditionally eaten on Shabbat and many Jewish holidays, into a warm, familiar breakfast dish. Challah itself symbolizes celebration, rest, and the blessings shared around the Shabbat table; its rich, egg-based dough and braided shape are closely associated with family meals and Jewish communal traditions. When leftover challah is used for French toast, it can also reflect the value of avoiding waste while extending the enjoyment of a meaningful food. Although French toast is not itself a religious ritual food, challah French toast can evoke memories of Shabbat, holidays, family gatherings, and Jewish home cooking.

ingredients:

  • 6 extra-large eggs
  • 1½ cups half-and-half or milk
  • 1 teaspoon grated orange zest
  • 1 tablespoon good honey
  • ½ teaspoon pure vanilla extract
  • 1 teaspoon kosher salt
  • 1 large loaf of challah or brioche bread
  • Unsalted butter
  • Vegetable oil
  • To serve:
  • Pure maple syrup

steps

  1. Preheat the oven to 250 degrees.
  2. In a large shallow dish, whisk together the eggs, half-and-half, orange zest, honey, vanilla, and salt. Slice the challah in 3/4-inch-thick slices. Soak as many slices in the egg mixture as possible for 5 minutes, turning once.
  3. Heat 1 tablespoon butter and 1 tablespoon oil in a very large sauté pan over medium heat. Add the soaked bread and cook for 2 to 3 minutes on each side, until nicely browned. Place the cooked French toast on a sheet pan and keep it warm in the oven. Fry the remaining soaked bread slices, adding butter and oil as needed, until it’s all cooked. Serve hot with maple syrup, raspberry preserves, and/or confectioners’ sugar.
